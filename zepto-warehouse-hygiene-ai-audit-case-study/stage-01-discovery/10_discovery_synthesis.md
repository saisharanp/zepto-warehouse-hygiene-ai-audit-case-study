# Stage 01 — Discovery Synthesis

The [evidence coding sheet](11_evidence_coding_sheet.md) provides the structured sample behind this synthesis; the [Stage 00 evidence ledger](../stage-00-research-setup/01_evidence_claim_ledger.md) controls claim strength and limitations.

## Evidence-backed findings

1. **Repeated control categories appeared across reported Hyderabad locations.** July reports described dirty racks/floors/fixtures, food stored on the ground, waste, weak food/non-food segregation, pest evidence, and missing or poorly displayed records.
2. **Enforcement is a lagging control.** The Narsingi stop-order can contain risk, but the public record does not establish how long conditions existed or what happened between inspections.
3. **Complaints can be the first signal.** Siasat reported that a customer complaint preceded the Mallepally inspection. Complaints should become risk triggers, not the only detection channel.
4. **Current evidence is location-specific.** Multiple reports support prioritising investigation; they do not establish Zepto-wide prevalence.
5. **Evidence provenance is part of trust.** A credible system needs the finding, remediation, re-check, reviewer, and decision—not only a green status.

## Evidence matrix

| Location | Reported evidence | Implication | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narsingi | Dirty racks, crates, floors, walls and ceilings; food on ground; waste; weak segregation; unclean air curtains; pest-unit positioning; temporary order halt | Visible controls can degrade enough to require immediate containment | High/medium |
| Malkajgiri | Food on floor, damaged flooring, houseflies, poor waste disposal, food/non-food co-storage, missing pest-control and medical-fitness records; reported 67% score | Visual hygiene and documentation failures can coexist | Medium |
| Mallepally | Cockroach and rodent evidence; inspection reportedly followed a customer complaint | Pest-control risk can reach customer-facing inventory and complaints can be leading indicators | Medium |

## Journey pain points

| Journey moment | Customer-facing failure | Operational gap | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receiving | Damaged or expired stock enters saleable inventory | Inconsistent intake/expiry controls | Link checks to receiving, batch and quarantine |
| Storage | Food is on dirty floors, near waste, or exposed to pests | Central team lacks frequent evidence | Guided zone walkthrough and risk triggers |
| Picking | Worker selects from a compromised zone | Store may remain orderable during remediation | Hold affected zone/SKU/category |
| Delivery | Customer receives a product that appears unsafe | Complaint becomes the first alert | Use complaints/returns to trigger unscheduled checks |
| Recovery | Refund resolves money, not confidence | No visible root-cause closure | Remediation timeline and independent re-check |

## Discovery limitations

- Published reports may describe different inspection dates or reuse official statements.
- No denominator exists for stores, orders, checks, or affected items.
- No internal Zepto response, baseline, or remediation data was available.
- Video cannot prove microbiological safety or every cold-chain condition.

## Stage 01 decision gate

**Decision:** Proceed to define and prioritize.

**Recommendation:** Solve the gap between inspections: frequent, verifiable detection connected to containment and remediation.

**Evidence:** Repeated July 2026 categories across three reported Hyderabad locations; confidence medium to high depending source.

**Open questions:** Whether the pattern is regional, licensee-specific, or network-wide, and what Zepto’s internal baseline shows.

**Approval request:** Approve the evidence-backed opportunity framing and move to problem definition and prioritization.
